# Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam Intl (FIMP)

*Pilots taking part in events that depart from or arrive at FIMP should make themselves familiar with this briefing page as well as with all existing charts.*

## Overview

- Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International is Mauritius' primary international airport.
  Capable of supporting Airbus A380 operations, it is the hub for Air Mauritius and the main gateway to the island.

- The airport has runway 14/32

## Departure Briefing

### Clearance Delivery

- FIMP does not have a real world delivery position.
  **FIMP_TWR** is responsible for IFR clearances, though a VATSIM Delivery position may be created during busy events.

- When requesting IFR clearance, please provide your **Aircraft Type**, **Stand Number** and **ATIS letter.**

- Be aware that there is high terrain west of the aerodrome.
  Certain departure procedures have specific climb performance requirements.
  As the pilot, you are responsible to ensure your aircraft is able to meet those requirements (beware of heavy payloads).
  If unable to meet them, notify the controller.

### Departure Procedures

- Comply with your assigned departure procedure and do not climb above your initial Climb Altitude unless instructed so.

- You may be given intermediate climb instructions due to traffic.
  Do not climb above any assigned flight level.

- Comply with any speed restrictions if provided.
  They are important to maintain adequate separation between traffic.

- You may be given a runway heading as a departure procedure, particularly if departing runway 14, which is the preferred runway for departures.

- When approaching the runway, you'll be asked to report fully ready for departure.
  That means that when given line-up or take-off clearance, you're expected to be ready to do so without delay.
  If you need more time, please advise the tower.

### Ground Operations

- **FIMP_TWR** will be the position responsible for providing push-back and taxiing instructions.

- Be aware that if you require full length for take-off for runway 14, you must request it.
  Otherwise, you'll be given an intersection departure from holding point A.

- Remember to squawk **Mode C** before taxiing.

- If told to monitor a frequency, you're expected to tune in the frequency but not to contact the controller.
  They will contact you when able.

### Code F Restrictions

- Taxiing for Code F aircraft should be conducted with caution.
  Consult charts for specific taxiway width restrictions.

### Departure Timeline

- For Cross Africa, your slot time is represented as a **CTOT (Calculated Take off Time).**
  You'll be expected to push-back at **TOBT (Target Off Block Time)** between 15 and 20 minutes before **CTOT (depending on how the traffic is flowing at that time).**

- As far as clearance is concerned, you can request it at any time before **TOBT**, as long as you'll be ready for push-back at **TOBT.**
  We suggest you request your clearance at least 15 minutes before **TOBT.**

- You should not need to connect to the network more than 60 minutes before your **CTOT.**

## Arrival Briefing

### Arrivals and Approaches

- As your flight is approaching FIMP, please make yourself familiar with the arrival and approach procedures for Mauritius.

- All pilots expect Runway 14 for landing, depending on wind conditions (Runway 32 may also be in use).

- Be aware that FIMP has distinct sets of approaches (ILS, RNAV and VOR) and that they have distinct approach procedures.
  Controllers will inform you what approach to expect based on an assigned arrival.
  Most likely, during busy events, expect the ILS approach.

- Be mindful that FIMP's ILS for runway 14 has a glide scope gradient of 3.5º, which is steeper than the standard 3º.
  Be prepared to adjust your descent rate accordingly.
  The RNP Z for runway 14 has a 3.3º glide scope gradient.

- FIMP is a single runway airport, unable to support High Intensity Runway Operations (HIRO), so during events, you should expect to hold.

- In case of missed approach, climb 4000 ft and maintain runway heading.
  You'll receive further instructions by the controllers.

- On initial contact with Mauritius Approach or Tower, you will be told the landing runway and the arrival.

- During busy events, expect to vacate right on D if landing runway 14 or vacate left on A if landing 32.

### Parking and Ground Operations

- When landing, pilots are advised to vacate as quickly as possible to allow for smooth operation.
  Backtracking may be required if landing deep on Runway 14 or 32, as the main taxiway does not extend to the full end of Runway 14.

## Oceanic Operations

- For Mauritius Oceanic flights, you will not be required to request an oceanic clearance.

- You will be requested however to maintain your flight level and an assigned mach number.

- During Oceanic En-route, you'll be able to request higher flight levels, depending on availability.

- Position Reports will not be required. You may be requested from oceanic controllers to provide your ETO (Expected Time Overhead) for a specific waypoint.
  What the controller is expecting is your time (as in, 1615z) at the specific waypoint.

- Remain on your last ATC assigned squawk code until instructed otherwise.

- CPDLC may be available at Mauritius Oceanic, but its availability is up to the controller.

- SELCAL is also available at Mauritius Oceanic.

- SLOP (Strategic Lateral Offset Procedure) is available at Mauritius Oceanic.
